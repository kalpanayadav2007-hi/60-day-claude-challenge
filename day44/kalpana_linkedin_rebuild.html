Part 1 — The Roast

what a recruiter actually thinks in the first 3 seconds

Headline 4/10

"BTech CSE Student | Cyber Security Honours | Open Source Contributor @ gssoc'26 | Shefi'15 scholar | DSA Learner | Exploring Full Stack"

❌ The problem

Six labels, zero throughline. A recruiter can't tell in one glance what you actually do — student, security person, open-source contributor, blockchain scholar, DSA grinder, and "exploring" full stack, all at once. "Exploring" is a hedge word — it tells them you don't consider yourself competent yet, even though your actual project work (a deployed MERN e-commerce app) says otherwise.

🧠 Why it hurts

Recruiters skim headlines for a role + a signal of strength. A list of six unrelated affiliations reads as "still figuring out my identity," not "here's my lane, and I'm good at it."

🔍 Invisible cost

This headline is also what shows up next to every comment and post you make — so all that hackathon activity is currently being introduced by "Exploring Full Stack," undercutting posts where you clearly did real, competent work.

About — First 2 Lines 0/10

❌ The problem

There is no About section at all.

🧠 Why it hurts

The first two lines of About are the most-read text on the entire profile (before "see more"). Right now that real estate shows nothing — LinkedIn just falls back to your latest post preview, which recruiters didn't come here to read.

🔍 Invisible cost

You have a genuinely good story sitting unused: a shipped full-stack project, a top-20,000-of-300,000 hackathon result as team lead, and an AI agent project with a real technical insight (memory > better prompts). None of it is summarized anywhere a recruiter would see it in under 10 seconds.

About — Full Section 0/10

❌ The problem

Same as above — blank. This is currently the single biggest gap on the whole profile, bigger than any wording issue elsewhere.

Experience 3/10

"Designer — hack with india · Full-time · May 2026–Present · 4 mos · Hybrid" — no bullets

❌ The problem

A title with zero description. "Designer" also undersells what you told me you actually make (certificates and event banners) — as written it could mean anything from UI design to graphic design to product design, and recruiters won't guess in your favor.

🧠 Why it hurts

Empty experience bullets are read as either "nothing to say" or "didn't bother." Both are worse than the truth.

🔍 Invisible cost

This is also your only listed role, so it's doing 100% of the "have you worked before" signaling on your whole profile — and right now it's doing that job with zero content.

Skills & Keywords 6/10

❌ The problem

Your 18 listed skills are a real mix of strong signal (MongoDB, JavaScript, HTML5, CSS, Front-End Development, Data Structures, Prompt Engineering) and generic office-tool noise (Microsoft Word, Microsoft Excel, Editing, Video Editing) that dilutes an otherwise technical profile. There's no visible priority — the top 3 pinned skills matter most for search, and right now that's whatever LinkedIn defaulted to.

🧠 Why it hurts

Recruiter search on LinkedIn is keyword-matched. Mixing "MS Excel" with "MongoDB" in the same list doesn't help either signal — it just makes the profile read as unfocused rather than specialized.

🔍 Invisible cost

This is actually your best-scoring section — the raw material (MERN stack, DSA, prompt engineering) is solid. The fix here is curation and ordering, not a rewrite from scratch.

26/100

Overall Profile Strength — mostly dragged down by a blank About and content-free Experience, not by lack of real material.

Part 4 — 7-Day LinkedIn Activation Plan

edits alone don't move the needle — activity does

DAY 1

Make all profile changes

  • Paste in new headline (pick one of the 3 options)
  • Paste in the rewritten About section
  • Add bullets to the Designer experience entry
  • Reorder skills: pin JavaScript, Front-End Development, MongoDB
  • Remove/deprioritize MS Word, MS Excel, Video Editing from skills
DAY 2

Publish Post #1 — "I just rebuilt my LinkedIn"

I just spent an hour rewriting my LinkedIn — and realized my About section had been blank this whole time. 😅 Turns out the biggest thing holding my profile back wasn't my experience, it was that I never actually wrote it down. If you're a student and your About section is empty right now — go check. Mine was, until today. What's one thing on your profile you've been putting off fixing? #LinkedInTips #StudentDeveloper #BuildInPublic
DAY 3

Send 10 strategic connection requests

Target: Full-Stack & AI engineers at companies you're interested in, GSSoC'26 mentors/fellow contributors, and Adobe/ViCoDathon hackathon organizers or teammates you haven't connected with yet.

Hi [Name], I really enjoyed [specific: your talk on X / building alongside you at ViCoDathon]. I'm a CSE student building full-stack + exploring AI — would love to stay connected.
DAY 4

Comment on 5 posts in your target space

Formula: Agree + Add insight + Ask a question. E.g. agree with their point, add one thing from your own experience (like the memory-vs-prompts lesson from your farm AI project), then ask a genuine follow-up question — this is what actually starts DMs, not generic "Great post!" comments.

DAY 5

Publish Post #2 — a lessons-learned post

The most useful thing I learned building an AI agent this year wasn't about prompts. It was that my farm advisory bot kept repeating the same bad suggestions — because it had no memory of what happened after it gave advice. Once I started storing decision → context → outcome instead of just generating answers, everything changed. It stopped saying "prices may rise" and started saying "in 4 similar cases, waiting led to losses." Better prompts help. Memory helps more. #AI #LearningInPublic #BuildingWithAI
DAY 6

Engage + send 5 more connection requests

Reply to every comment on Day 5's post within a few hours — early replies are what LinkedIn's algorithm rewards with more reach. Send 5 more targeted requests using the Day 3 template.

DAY 7

Review profile views & connection acceptance rate

Check Settings → "Who's viewed your profile" and your connection request acceptance rate. A jump in profile views after Day 1–2 confirms the headline/About change is working. A low acceptance rate on connection requests (under ~30%) means the Day 3 message needs to get more specific, not friendlier.
